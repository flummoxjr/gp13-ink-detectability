# 3. Two screens, two nulls with numbers attached: dual-energy chemistry on PHerc.Paris.4, and the ink_3d transfer failure on PHerc1203

This section reports two whole-volume screens that both returned negative results, and treats the negatives as results. Part A (§3.1–3.5) is a dual-energy X-ray screen of PHerc.Paris.4 for heavy-metal ink, run on a Pb-K-edge-straddling energy pair (the published PHerc.0332 dual-energy null used 53/70 keV, entirely below the edge), including an explicit, referee-checked statement of what its clean null does and does not exclude. Part B (§3.6–3.11) is the full-band ink_3d screen of PHerc1203 that failed by domain shift, characterized quantitatively enough to become a fine-tune recipe and a fixed benchmark rather than a write-off.

These two screens ask questions the rest of the report cannot. The Paris 4 dual-energy pair straddles lead's K-edge, which is a cheap chemistry channel that nobody had signed correctly for lead — if metallic ink were there, it should show up without any model at all. And running the released volumetric model over PHerc1203's 2.4 µm band was the only honest way to find out whether it transfers before trusting anything it says. Both came back negative, and both negatives are worth more written down than left as an assumption.

## 3.1 Part A — why dual-energy, and the sign lesson that nearly sank it

**Data.** BM18 scanned PHerc.Paris.4 twice at 45.532 µm within ~90 minutes on the same mount: nominal 74 keV and 110 keV filtered-white ("pink") beams (scans `20260310152857` / `20260310160232`; the 110 keV volume was acquired under the radix "…_109keV_B" and labeled 110.0 keV — one of several metadata inconsistencies catalogued in `qc/k3_stage1_review.md`, W7). Both are uint8 exports with known linear windows (µ₇₄ ∈ [−0.058, 0.270], µ₁₁₀ ∈ [−0.040, 0.200] cm⁻¹, verified against the reconstruction metadata to <1e−4 twice over), both Paganin-filtered at delta_beta = 10.

**The physics that makes the pair informative.** The K-edges of every classic heavy-metal ink candidate sit *between* the two beam energies: Au 80.7, Hg 83.1, **Pb 88.005**, Bi 90.5 keV. Below its K-edge (74 keV) Pb attenuates less than above it (110 keV): µ/ρ = 2.95 vs 4.37 cm²/g, so µ₇₄/µ₁₁₀(Pb) ≈ **0.674** — far *below* the papyrus baseline (measured per-voxel median 1.248), while every mid-Z element (Ca, Fe, …, K-edges < 74 keV) sits *above* it (Ca ≈ 1.4, Fe ≈ 2.25). The two tails of the ratio distribution are two different chemistry channels, and heavy metals are uniquely *signed* by direction — unlike the published PHerc.0332 53/70 keV screen, where Fe (2.03) is degenerate with Pb (2.04) in the same high tail.

**The sign lesson.** Our stage-1 screen got this wrong: it flagged only the *high*-ratio tail as the "dense ink" channel — a channel that can never find lead. The adversarial QC audit (`qc/k3_stage1_review.md`) caught the sign error before scaling, and additionally showed the stage-1 headline statistic (dense-tail ratio 1.705 vs papyrus 1.248, n = 3,196 / 4,790,408 L3 voxels) was inflated by mask-rim contamination (36% of dense voxels within 2 voxels of the mask edge vs 1.0% of papyrus — a 37× over-representation) and numerator selection bias (selecting on the denominator instead gives 1.46); the honest interior co-bright value is ≈ 1.5–1.6, a genuine but *mid-Z* dense phase. The audit also flagged uncalibrated per-volume zero offsets (~10% systematic on the absolute ratio) and alignment validated only at 8×-binned resolution. All four blockers were fixed before the full screen ran. We report the catch because it is the method: a K-edge sign error in a dual-energy screen produces a confident, well-populated detection channel pointed at the wrong chemistry.

## 3.2 Stage-2 method: the corrected screen

Script: `k3_stage2_screen.py`; outputs `out/k3_s2_stats.json`, `out/k3_s2_{low,high}_clusters.json`. The screen runs at level 1 (91.064 µm voxels) over the full scroll, slab-streamed (256-slice slabs + 2-slice halo, volumes held as uint8, ~8 GB peak RAM). Per the QC blockers:

- **Air-offset zero calibration per volume**: measured on interior sub-papyrus voxels, −0.0310 cm⁻¹ (74 keV) and −0.0268 cm⁻¹ (110 keV). Both are *negative* where air should read ≈ 0 — consistent with residual phase-fringe undershoot at delta_beta = 10 — so the offset correction is itself uncertain, and all sensitivity numbers below are quoted in two bracketing calibration frames.
- **Sub-voxel alignment verified, volumes used native**: residual translation between the two reconstructions measured at 0.017 voxel, below the pre-set 0.05-voxel gate, so no resampling was applied (resampling would have blurred the 74/110 ratio at sheet boundaries — the thing being measured).
- **Both chemistry channels**: after 3-voxel mask erosion and papyrus gating, the per-voxel ratio is aggregated over 3³ neighborhoods (≥14 papyrus voxels required), detrended per 32-slice sub-block against radius (the QC-measured +4% z-drift and +2% radial trend from source ramp and beam hardening), giving a detrended statistic with measured σ = **0.1259**. LOW channel = detrended ratio < −4σ (heavy metals: Pb/Au/Hg/Bi); HIGH channel = > +4σ (mid-Z: Ca/Fe). Both channels additionally require co-brightness (both energies above their papyrus p75: 0.1171 / 0.0998 cm⁻¹) so that low-density partial-volume voxels cannot fire on ratio noise.

## 3.3 The LOW-channel result: a clean null, and exactly what it excludes

**Result: 2 singleton voxels fired in ~2.7×10⁸ tested aggregates** (`out/k3_s2_stats.json`; the n is consistent with the L3 papyrus count × 64 minus erosion, not independently re-derived). A Gaussian 4σ tail on that n predicts ~8,488 hits; observing 2 means the detrended statistic is strongly sub-Gaussian in its left tail. There is no Pb-signature anomaly anywhere in the screened scroll, including the title region.

**The honest sensitivity statement** (full derivation, closed-form model, and a hostile referee pass in `out/k3_sensitivity_bound.md` + `.json`, computation in `k3_sensitivity_calc.py`): the null is *clean but nearly vacuous as a lead exclusion*.

- The 4σ criterion demands a ratio depression of 0.504. Computed from the exact mixture model (not the linearization, which is invalid at this amplitude), **no physically realizable Pb configuration reaches it**: the maximum response is 2.8–3.4σ depending on calibration frame, and that only for ink pervading an entire 273 µm aggregation neighborhood. Realistic single-surface stroke geometry (ink fraction d ≈ 0.33–0.5 of the neighborhood) caps at **1.1–1.7σ**.
- The uint8 export windows clip the 110 keV channel at a Pb areal density of ≈ 311 µg/cm² per voxel column; beyond that the ratio *rises again*, re-crossing the papyrus baseline near 576 µg/cm² and pegging at 1.35 once both channels clip (≳ 639 µg/cm²) — i.e. **heavily loaded Pb ink exits the low tail entirely and is flagged by neither channel**. A saturation channel (co-occurring u8 = 255) was not implemented and is a necessary follow-up before any "no dense metallic ink" claim.
- The Pb loadings actually measured in Herculaneum ink letters — 84 ± 5 µg/cm² (large fragment) and 16 ± 5 µg/cm² (small fragment); Tack et al. 2016, Brun et al. 2016; ESRF ID21 XRF on Institut de France fragments — would have produced ≈ 1.0σ and 0.3σ at realistic stroke geometry. Even the most favorable re-reading of the data (3σ, raw frame) opens only a narrow window, σ_Pb ≈ 203–343 µg/cm² at ≥ 88% neighborhood coverage — **2.4× and 13× above the two published values**.

The defensible public sentence, verbatim from the bound memo: *"the Paris 4 74/110 keV screen excludes only lead loadings an order of magnitude beyond those ever measured in Herculaneum ink; it does not test the Brun/Tack metallic-ink hypothesis."* This supersedes our own stage-1 QC estimate ("realistic Pb ink > 5σ"), which neglected ratio saturation and window clipping.

What the null *does* establish, and why it was worth the run:

1. **False-positive calibration.** 2 hits against a Gaussian expectation of ~8,488 is a direct measurement that the detrended-ratio statistic and its threshold produce essentially zero false positives at scroll scale — the prerequisite for the upgraded screen in §3.5 to produce a short reviewable candidate list instead of thousands.
2. **A validated method.** Sign-correct two-channel design, per-volume offset calibration, native sub-voxel alignment, trend removal, and slab-streaming at full-scroll scale are now demonstrated on real BM18 dual-energy data, with the failure modes (§3.4) characterized.
3. **The referee pass as a result.** The sensitivity memo was independently re-derived by a hostile referee: the Pb sign, mixture algebra, σ propagation, and all raw-frame numbers reproduced; six errors were found and fixed (post-clip re-cross wrong by ~1.7×, an internally inconsistent corrected-frame baseline, a misdescribed Tack quantification model, a 2σ/3σ window mix-up, a self-contradictory caveat, an understated Fe degeneracy). The corrected-frame numbers moved by ≤ 25% and no conclusion changed. Residual uncertainties (±5–20% on corrected-frame areal densities from 2-s.f. stage-1 medians; pink-beam ±10% systematics; the modeled-not-measured gate floor of ≈ 70–170 µg/cm²) are enumerated in the memo and only strengthen the "nearly vacuous as exclusion" reading, since every quoted σ ceiling is an upper bound.

![Top five HIGH-channel clusters at L0: 74 keV (top row), 110 keV (middle), papyrus-masked 74/110 ratio (bottom). #1/#2/#4 are compact nodular inclusions, co-bright in both energies with locally elevated ratio — human-verified as dense material; the mid-Z (Ca/Fe-like) attribution is inferred from the ratio sign only, not measured chemistry. #3/#5 show fiber-scale red/blue ratio striping with no corresponding dense object — the PSF-mismatch signature.](../figures/k3_s2_high_verify.png)

**#1/#2/#4 — the incrustation catalog.** Nodular, mm-scale, co-bright dense inclusions; #1/#2 sit in the same flank region (L0 z ≈ 1240) as the interior dense phase the stage-1 QC had already isolated after artifact removal (six coherent components, largest ≈ 12.7 mm³, ratio 1.48–1.58, 2.5–3 mm below the surface). The clump geometry does not resemble writing; the ratio sign is mid-Z. The defensible label is mineral incrustation or debris (Ca/Fe-family), not any ink.

**#3/#5 — artifact family 1: PSF mismatch.** These two clusters are not objects but streaks: fiber-scale high/low ratio striping. The mechanism is that with equal delta_beta the Paganin filter width scales as √λ, so the 74 keV volume is ~22% more blurred than the 110 keV volume; at every sharp papyrus/air interface the ratio numerator and denominator have different edge profiles, striping the ratio field along fibers. The quantitative test (`comb/comb_k3_psfmatch*.py`, `comb/k3_psfmatch*.json`): blur the 110 keV channel with a small Gaussian and find the σ that minimizes the 4σ-excess population. At σ_eq = **0.62 px (#3)** and **0.56 px (#5)** the hot-voxel count collapses **5220 → 958 (5.4×)** and **3477 → 967 (3.6×)**, and the elongated streak components shrink and de-elongate (largest component #3: 555 → 103 vox, elongation 16.9 → 11.7; #5: 163 → 39 vox, 7.7 → 4.5). A sub-voxel resolution-matching blur removes most of the "detection": these are point-spread artifacts, not material.

![PSF-matching sweep on clusters #3 and #5: local ratio excess (hot = excess > 0.504, the stage-2 4σ criterion) as the 110 keV channel is blurred with σ = 0 → 1.0 px. The hot population collapses 3.6–5.4× at σ_eq ≈ 0.56–0.62 px and the streaks fragment — the signature of PSF mismatch, not chemistry.](../figures/k3_psfmatch2.png)

**Artifact family 2: patchy regional ratio variation.** After PSF matching, the #3/#5 windows still carry elevated whole-window median ratios (1.48 / 1.46), which raised a "regional elevation at z ≈ 3900–4020" flag. A 28-window matched null killed it (`comb/comb_skeptic_k3.json`): rings of windows at the clusters' exact radius and z (plus ±z planes), identical pipeline. The null distribution of whole-window medians spans **1.17–1.52** (IQR 1.26–1.38); **5/28 null windows ≥ 1.43**, and two windows on the same ring and z as #3 reach 1.52 — above both flagged values. A pre-selected top-cluster window sitting at ~p80 of its own matched base rate is nothing. The dual-energy ratio field of the lower scroll is patchy at the ±0.15 level everywhere at these radii.

**The caution for future dual-energy work**: on Paganin-reconstructed pink-beam pairs, the mid-Z channel's false-positive budget is dominated by (1) fiber-scale PSF-mismatch striping — pre-equalize the PSFs (σ ≈ 0.6 px here) or any per-voxel ratio work at sheet scale is compromised — and (2) ±0.15-level regional ratio patchiness, so no regional anomaly claim is meaningful without a matched-radius/z base-rate distribution. The 28-window null table is the reusable reference distribution for this scan pair.

## 3.5 What a real Pb bound would take

The gap to Tack/Brun-level sensitivity is ≈ 5× in σ at stroke scale (need σ_ratio ≈ 0.02–0.03 per matched aggregate vs 0.126 now). The concrete upgrade path, from the bound memo: (i) re-screen at L0/L1 with a *sheet-surface-conformal* matched filter — aggregate along the writing surface only, removing the d ≈ 0.33–0.5 stroke-dilution penalty and averaging 10²–10³ voxels per stroke; (ii) recalibrate zero offsets on resolved air; (iii) add the saturation channel; (iv) replace the p75 co-brightness gate (which specifically disfavors the partial-volume-dimmed sheet-surface voxels where ink lives, flooring sensitivity at ≈ 70–170 µg/cm²) with a surface-band mask; (v) treat 2–2.5σ candidates as a ranked shortlist for visual review rather than applying a hard 4σ cut — affordable precisely because §3.3 measured the false-positive rate. Under that design, Tack-level ink (84 µg/cm²) becomes a ~5σ event. I offer this as a community recommendation rather than claim it as planned work — beamtime-grade calibration is not something I have — but I will gladly run the analysis side on any scan pair the team produces.

## 3.6 Part B — the ink_3d full-band screen on PHerc1203: setup and the stop decision

I ran the released model on PHerc1203's 2.4 µm band before considering anything more expensive, on the principle that you should find out what the free option does before paying for the alternative. I was working against an $80 cloud budget for the month, which is also why the gate that stopped this run at $4.46 mattered more than the run did.

The screen: `scrollprize/ink_3d_dino_guided` (checkpoint v3-78k-fullsup, trained on PHerc.Paris.4), run over the PHerc1203 2.4 µm masked band in 256³ tiles (stride 256) on a six-pod RTX 5090 fleet (burn $3.49/hr). A live QC agent ran a five-control battery at ~45% coverage and returned a stop verdict; the fleet was terminated at **$4.46 of the $80 cap**, with 29,748 unique scored tiles across 14 z-slabs secured (`salvage/tiles.parquet`; the QC round itself saw the first 3,346). Full verdict: `qc_live/round1_verdict.md`.

**The result in one sentence: the screen output is the model's genuine response to the volume, the pipeline is verified faithful end-to-end, and that response is an unrankable scroll-wide blanket — a quantified cross-scroll transfer failure, not a pipeline bug and not text.** No model detection in this part is called ink or letters; nothing here was human-verified as either.

One evidence line from this scorecard was later retired by our own full-data analysis, and we report that too: the QC round's "top-1% tiles are spatially anti-clustered (noise)" claim was a coverage/small-n artifact. On all 29,748 tiles the raw top-1% is strongly *clustered* (mean NN 2.13 tile units vs 3.14 ± 0.08 random, z = −12.1); thinning the same slabs back to QC's n = 3,346 reproduces adjacency values spanning 0.00–0.45 across 50 thinnings, containing QC's observed 0.0 (`salvage/scalar_report.md` §4). The stop decision survives on the other grounds (blanket level, zero silent tiles, morphology); the specific evidence line does not, and is retired.

## 3.8 Mechanism: the model paints sheet surfaces

The salvage morphology pass (`salvage/morph_report.md`; 63 sampled probability maps matched to CT and the released surface prediction; sample explicitly top-pmax/edge-biased — rates below characterize the sampled morphology, not unbiased scroll-wide rates) revised the round-1 "crack-follower" reading:

- **79% of fired voxels sit in giant (≥2000 vox) thin sheet-conformal membranes** — local thickness 2.6 vox mean / 4.0 vox p90 (≈ 25–40 µm), 21% of large components spanning >90% of their tile. Genuine through-sheet crack ribbons are **0.2%** of fired voxels. The failure mode is *sheet-surface painting*, wholesale.
- Component planes align with local sheet normals: median angle 17.5° vs 50.2° ± 2.0 (across-tile shuffle), 59.8° ± 2.2 (isotropic), 21.2° ± 1.0 (within-tile shuffle, z = −3.9); every alignment claim carries a ≥200-draw null. Ink predicts conformality — but so does this FP mode, so conformality has zero discriminative power here.
- The only ink-plausible shape class ("patch": flat, conformal, stroke-thickness) is 6.3% of fired voxels, has round footprints (in-plane aspect median 1.54 — flecks, not strokes), matches the membranes in thickness and mean probability, appears in 25/40 usable tiles rather than any localized region, and dominates **0 of 63 tiles** (max patch fraction 0.33).

Firing traces sheet surfaces and void boundaries as conformal bands and halos; no panel contains glyph-shaped structure. ## 3.9 The covariate shift, quantified — and the histogram-matching refutation

**Intensity shift (measured, insufficient as explanation).** Nonzero-CT percentiles [p1, p25, p50, p75, p99]: Paris 4 = [26, 49, 91, 116, 158]; the probed 1203 tile = [16, 39, 47, 103, 196] — a much darker median (47 vs 91 DN) with a longer bright tail, a distribution-*shape* shift that percentile min-max normalization cannot correct.

**The controlled test.** Histogram-match a 1203 tile to the Paris 4 nonzero-CT distribution (matching verified: matched p1/p50/p99 = 25/96/154 vs target 26/97/155) and re-infer: f05 **0.0392 → 0.0810 — firing doubled**, exceeding the in-domain ink-active level (0.0695). Giving 1203's texture Paris 4's intensity profile makes it look *more* Paris4-ink-like to the model. This refutes the entire input-side intensity-adaptation family (histmatch, mean/std alignment, percentile tweaks) in one experiment: the domain shift lives at texture/morphology level.

**What actually drives the firing** (`salvage/scalar_report.md`, all claims with ≥200-permutation nulls, block-permutation nulls at 4³-tile ≈ 2.5 mm scale wherever autocorrelation would inflate naive p): the dominant covariate is **mean CT density — Spearman ρ(meanCT, f05) = +0.73**, subsuming fill (0.38). A four-covariate model (fill + meanCT + z + winding radius) reaches **R² = 0.62** on f05; a flexible variant adds only 0.006. Geography is structured but small: firing is flat through the body and falls ~2.5× in the outermost wraps (radial η² = 0.067 vs block null 0.021 ± 0.004), angular η² = 0.023 (null 0.005 ± 0.002), z marginal (η² = 0.016 vs 0.012 ± 0.002, p = 0.010, confounded with sparse-slab coverage). The in-plane f05 field visibly traces the wrap spiral — because the density field does. The residual ~36% of variance retains genuine spatial structure (Moran's I = 0.289 vs block null 0.213 ± 0.0025, z = +31; top-1% residual tiles cluster at mean NN 2.10 vs 2.39 ± 0.06 block null) but its hotspots are elongated axis-aligned sheet/crack-like runs, it correlates with no independently measured condition proxy, and it is parked as a cross-reference layer for a future working detector — structure ≠ ink, and nothing here validates it as anything.

## 3.10 The atlas that is not a damage atlas: sign inversion

The last salvage hypothesis — reframe the field as a community crackle/damage atlas — was refuted by its own sign (`salvage/reframe_report.md`, all-29,748-tile proxies, block-permutation nulls throughout). A damage atlas requires firing to be high where texture is rough and segmentation struggles. The opposite holds on every axis: interior-tile Spearman with crack-texture std = **−0.54**; with m7 surface-mask density = +0.42; with sheet recovery = +0.41; with mean density = +0.68. The model fires on dense, smooth, well-preserved, well-segmentable wraps and goes quiet on damage — a *preservation/density* readout. And it is strictly dominated by free statistics: mean CT (computable from the public zarr pyramid in seconds, no GPU) predicts m7 segmentation holes better than f05 (AUC 0.892 vs 0.855), and f05's incremental information about holes after cheap CT covariates is zero to measurement precision (partial ρ = −0.001 vs block null −0.012 ± 0.010; leave-slab-out ΔR² = 0.0000). The fleet compute bought a noisy copy of a free covariate. (Side finding with independent value: the released m7 surface predictions are binary {0,255} at every pyramid level — no confidence field exists in the release.)

The atlas was built anyway, with honest labeling (`salvage/atlas_1203.{npy,png}` + README): it documents the failure mode and fixes the baseline for §3.11 — it is not a condition tool.

## 3.11 The product: a fine-tune recipe and a fixed benchmark

The characterization converts directly into a training and acceptance recipe for anyone (including us) attempting a 1203-adapted ink_3d:

1. **Hard negatives are sheet surfaces, not cracks.** The FP mode is painting *intact* sheet surfaces (79% membranes, 0.2% crack ribbons; firing *anti*-correlates with damage at ρ = −0.54). Mine hard-negative patches from PHerc1203's dense, well-preserved sheet stacks — the dark cells of the atlas — not from damage zones. Corollary with a number: on-sheet gating in post-processing can never rescue this output (the FPs are already on-sheet; bulk on/off-sheet contrast 0.64–1.62×).
2. **Fine-tune, don't adapt inputs.** Input-side intensity adaptation is refuted (f05 doubled under verified histogram matching, §3.9). The viable path is scroll-specific fine-tuning of the v3-78k checkpoint with 1203 patches in the loader per the PHerc1667 pseudo-label playbook (the training config already supports self-distilled dynamic labels), seeded with the hard negatives above.
3. **Acceptance gate before any fleet.** The five-control battery of §3.7 (pipeline equivalence, morphology vs surface prediction, tail discrimination, in-domain distribution reference, single-tile adaptation probe) runs on ~30 tiles for a few dollars. The round-1 fleet spent $4.46 to learn what this gate now detects up front; no candidate model gets fleet budget without passing it.
4. **The before/after baseline is frozen.** `salvage/tiles.parquet` + `salvage/proxies.parquet` + `salvage/atlas_1203.npy` define the current model's behavior exactly. A successful fine-tune must (a) produce silent tiles on 1203 (the in-domain signature: 5/8 random Paris 4 blocks at f05 = 0.000 vs 0 silent tiles in 29,748 here), (b) break the density dependence (ρ(f05, meanCT) = 0.73 → near 0), and (c) fire, if at all, in stroke-shaped rather than membrane-shaped components (§3.8 census as reference). Anything less is the same failure with new weights.

The screen found nothing, and I think the frozen benchmark and the hard-negative recipe it produced will outlast the screen itself — which is not the outcome I wanted, but it is the useful one.
